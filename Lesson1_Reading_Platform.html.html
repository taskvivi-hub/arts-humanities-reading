Paragraph 1 · Introduction

1. The Edinburgh Festival Fringe is a world-renowned festival that combines many arts and cultural festivals taking place in Edinburgh, Scotland.

2. Each summer, mostly in August, artists from all over the world will gather together here to present their hard work.

3. Taipei First High School from Taiwan, for example, was invited to perform in the festival.

4. Though the festivals are put on by various organizations unrelated to each other, and so are officially separate events, they are regarded as part of the same event.

5. Collectively, they form the largest annual cultural festival in the world.

愛丁堡藝穗節是世界知名的節慶，結合了許多在蘇格蘭愛丁堡舉行的藝術與文化節慶。

每年夏天，主要是在八月，來自世界各地的藝術家會聚集在這裡，呈現他們努力完成的作品。

例如，來自臺灣的臺北第一高中曾受邀在這個節慶中演出。

雖然這些節慶是由彼此沒有關聯的不同組織所舉辦，在官方上屬於各自獨立的活動，但人們仍將它們視為同一個大型活動的一部分。

整體而言，這些活動共同形成了世界上規模最大的年度文化節慶。

History

1. The idea of the Festival originates from “providing a platform for the flowering of the human spirit” and enriching the cultural life of Scotland, Britain and Europe.

2. It was conceived in the wake of the Second World War.

3. Its founders included Rudolf Bing, the General Manager of Glyndebourne Opera Festival, Henry Harvey Wood, Head of the British Council in Scotland, Sidney Newman, as well as music gurus and civic leaders.

這個節慶的理念源自於「提供一個讓人類精神蓬勃發展的平台」，並豐富蘇格蘭、英國與歐洲的文化生活。

這個構想是在第二次世界大戰之後形成的。

創辦人包括格林德伯恩歌劇節總經理 Rudolf Bing、英國文化協會蘇格蘭分會主管 Henry Harvey Wood、Sidney Newman，以及音樂界專家與地方領袖。

Background

1. In 1999, the Festival opened a new central box office and information center in The Hub, a converted church on Castlehill, directly below Edinburgh Castle.

2. Originally built as the Tolbooth Church (1842-44) to house the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland, its tall Gothic spire is the highest point in central Edinburgh (outside of the Castle) and a landmark visible for miles around.

1999 年，節慶在 The Hub 設立了新的中央售票處與資訊中心。The Hub 是一座由教堂改建而成的建築，位於 Castlehill，就在愛丁堡城堡下方。

這座建築最初於 1842 至 1844 年間建為 Tolbooth Church，用來容納蘇格蘭教會大會。它高聳的哥德式尖塔是愛丁堡市中心除城堡之外的最高點，也是從數英里外就能看見的地標。

Performances

1. This world's largest arts festival had 25 days of events in 2012, totaling over 2,695 shows from 47 countries in 279 venues.

2. It is a showcase for the performing arts, particularly theatre and comedy (which has seen substantial growth in recent years), although dance and music are also represented.

3. In 2012, 36% of shows were comedy and 28% theatrical productions.

4. Theatrical productions range from the classics of ancient Greece to William Shakespeare, Samuel Beckett and contemporary works.

5. In 2012, 1,418 shows were having their world premieres.

2012 年，這個世界大型藝術節舉辦了 25 天的活動，共有來自 47 個國家的 2,695 多場演出，分布在 279 個場地。

這個節慶是表演藝術的重要展示舞台，尤其以戲劇與喜劇為主；近年來喜劇有大幅成長，同時舞蹈與音樂也包含在內。

2012 年，36% 的演出是喜劇，28% 是戲劇製作。

戲劇作品的範圍很廣，從古希臘經典作品、William Shakespeare、Samuel Beckett，到當代作品都有。

2012 年共有 1,418 場演出進行全球首演。

Business Model · Part 1

1. However, the world's greatest arts festival came to an end in 2012 and the news was splashed all over media.

2. The business model for show business related to artistic events makes it difficult to sustain this grand festival.

3. Firstly, artists all over the world are scrutinizing the quality of the performances and some feel that the hodgepodge of different performances is not synonymous with the so-called "art".

4. Secondly, the ticket price and sales model are not sustainable as a result of world economy.

5. The increasing costs associated with hiring large venues, theatre licenses and related costs and accommodation during the Edinburgh Festival are all barriers for both audiences and performers to actively engage in this Festival.

然而，課文指出這個大型藝術節在 2012 年結束，而且這項消息受到媒體廣泛報導。

與藝術活動相關的表演產業商業模式，使這個大型節慶難以長期維持。

Business Model · Part 2

1. In recent years, the Festival Committee adopted a new business model.

2. They commercialized the "Pay-To-Play" scheme for venues and charge artists in advance of the Fringe.

3. In many cases, venue costs such as venue rents and guarantees, compulsory marketing and various deductions mean that performers are being charged more than they can make back in ticket sales.

4. Stewart Lee, for example, criticized this model in The Guardian: "For decades, the Fringe has been a utopia for artists and performers – but now profit-obsessed promoters are tearing it to pieces."

近年來，節慶委員會採用了一種新的商業模式。

他們將場地的「付費演出」制度商業化，並在藝穗節開始前向藝術家收費。

在許多情況下，場地租金、保證款、強制行銷費與各種扣款，使表演者支付的費用可能高於他們從售票收入中賺回的金額。

例如，Stewart Lee 曾在《衛報》中批評這種模式，認為藝穗節數十年來一直是藝術家與表演者的理想天地，但現在過度追求利潤的主辦者正在破壞它。

Benefits and Future

1. Despite all the criticisms, the Festival has brought about many benefits to Edinburgh, particularly tourism.

2. It is certainly a great branding exercise for the country too.

3. The Festival provides ample networking opportunities for the performers around the world and it is an excellent training ground or springboard for future career advancement.

4. It is certainly an exciting and fun for performers as well as spectators.

5. So, who knows? With the world economy gradually resuscitating, maybe the Festival will have a renaissance again in the near future.

儘管受到許多批評，這個節慶仍為愛丁堡帶來許多好處，尤其是在觀光方面。

它也確實有助於建立並推廣這個國家的形象。

這個節慶為世界各地的表演者提供充足的建立人脈機會，也是累積經驗以及推動未來職涯發展的良好平台與跳板。

對表演者和觀眾來說，這個節慶確實既令人興奮又充滿樂趣。

所以，誰知道呢？隨著全球經濟逐漸恢復活力，也許這個節慶在不久的將來會再次迎來復興。
